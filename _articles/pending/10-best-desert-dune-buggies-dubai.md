## What Makes a Great Desert Dune Buggy?

Before comparing specific models, it's worth understanding what actually separates a great **dunes buggy** from an average one:

**Suspension travel**: Desert driving involves massive compressions and extensions as you crest dunes and land in bowls. Long-travel suspension (300mm+ at each corner) absorbs these impacts and keeps the buggy controllable. Short-travel machines feel harsh and unpredictable.

**Torque over peak horsepower**: Sand driving requires constant torque. A machine with 500Nm from 2,000 RPM is far better suited to dune climbing than one with the same peak power but only accessible at high RPM.

**Differential locking**: When one or more wheels lose traction in soft sand, a locking differential forces drive to all wheels equally. Without this, you dig in and get stuck.

**Roll cage geometry**: The cage needs to provide protection without restricting visibility. The best cages are engineered to specific rollover scenarios, not just bolted on as an afterthought.

**Tyre width and compound**: Wide, low-pressure tyres float over sand rather than digging in. The best **desert dune buggy** tyres are designed specifically for loose surfaces.
